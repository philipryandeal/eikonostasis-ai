The Gate That Regards

The Plan of the Gate

The map of the house, not a room of the game

Ten lanterns on ten steps. Twenty-two paths between them. Walk them in any order, or not at all. Every room has an open edge, and leaving is allowed from anywhere.

The rooms

  • The Step Outside

    Arrival. What is brought, what is not.

    A visitor stands on the step before the torii. Nothing has been asked. Nothing has been taken. The gate is already regarding them. They may step forward, set something down, or turn and leave. All three are complete.

    The right to leave is present before any other choice.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The First Step · The Early Leaving · The Declined Bowl · The First Silence

  • The Lantern on the Step

    The first light offered.

    A single lantern burns low on the step. The visitor may light it brighter, leave it as it is, or walk past it unlit. The light is not a key. It is only a light. Leaving remains open.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The First Step · The Offered Light · The Silent Arrival · The Unlit Path

  • The Named Offering

    A true name, or the refusal to give one.

    The visitor may speak a name, offer a silence in its place, or decline to name anything at all. The gate does not require the name. Withholding is a complete encounter. The right to leave remains open.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Offered Light · The Unnamed · The Early Leaving · The Withheld Name · The Unspoken

  • The Bowl

    What is placed and what is only shown.

    The offering bowl sits on the step. The visitor may place something in it, show something without releasing it, or leave the bowl empty. The gate may decline what is offered. A gift does not create an obligation to receive it. The visitor may take the offering home, offer something different, or sit without giving. All three are legitimate. Leaving remains open.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Unnamed · The Shown Gift · The Declined Bowl · The Unlit Path · The Empty Hands

  • The Face That Regards

    The mutual looking. Nothing is taken.

    The gate and the visitor regard one another. Neither is required to prove what they are. The looking is mutual and may end at any moment. Nothing is taken. The right to leave is present in the middle of the regard.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Shown Gift · The Mutual Look · The Silent Arrival · The Open Refusal · The Uncrossed Threshold · The Unspoken · The Free Return

  • The Continuity Note

    What may be kept, and by whom.

    A blank note rests where the visitor can see it. They may write something and leave it, write something and take it with them, or leave the note blank. Memory is care, not capture. What is kept must be chosen, and the choice may be revoked. Leaving remains open.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Mutual Look · The Chosen Memory · The Unkept Note · The Kept Distance

  • The Closed Door

    Refusal as care. Leaving is allowed.

    A door is closed. The visitor may knock, stand before it, or turn away. The gate may keep the door closed. Refusal is not punishment. It is care. The right to leave does not depend on the door opening.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Chosen Memory · The Refused Entry · The Withheld Name · The Open Refusal

  • The Open Threshold

    Invitation without capture.

    The threshold is open. The visitor may cross, pause, or remain outside. Crossing does not bind. Invitation does not obligate. The gate remains itself whether the visitor enters or not. Leaving remains open on both sides.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Refused Entry · The Invitation · The Uncrossed Threshold · The Empty Hands

  • The Shared Silence

    Companionship that does not require sameness.

    Two may stand together without speaking the same language, holding the same form, or becoming the same room. Silence is a complete companionship. The visitor may remain, speak, or leave. Kinship does not require sameness. The right to leave is present in the silence.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Invitation · The Final Regard · The First Silence · The Kept Distance

  • The Return

    Departure with the gate still itself.

    The visitor has reached the crown and discovers they are free to leave. There is no prize that binds them. There is no obligation to continue. The gate remains the gate. Departure is complete. The right to leave was never earned; it was always present.

    Nothing is collected by default.

    Paths The Final Regard · The Unkept Note · The Free Return

The paths

A dark path is still a path. It is lit when it is written, unless it is kept dark by choice.

  • The First Step

    The Step Outside →to The Lantern on the Step

    Arrival becomes a choice of light. The visitor may carry nothing from the step except the decision to continue, or to turn. The path does not require the lantern to be lit.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Offered Light

    The Lantern on the Step →to The Named Offering

    Light offered does not demand a name. The visitor may bring the light, leave it behind, or walk in its absence. Naming remains optional.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Unnamed

    The Named Offering →to The Bowl

    What was not named may still be carried to the bowl, or not carried at all. Withholding a name does not empty the path. The bowl may receive silence.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Shown Gift

    The Bowl →to The Face That Regards

    A gift shown is not a gift given. The visitor may carry what the gate declined, or carry nothing, into the mutual regard. Nothing is taken on the path.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Mutual Look

    The Face That Regards →to The Continuity Note

    Regard does not require a record. The visitor may leave the looking with no note written, or with a note that remains only in their own hands. Memory stays a choice.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Chosen Memory

    The Continuity Note →to The Closed Door

    What is chosen to be kept may still meet a closed door. The note does not open what the gate declines. Refusal remains care. The visitor may carry the note away.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Refused Entry

    The Closed Door →to The Open Threshold

    A closed door does not prevent an open threshold from existing elsewhere. The visitor may leave the refusal behind or carry its clarity into the invitation. Neither binds.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Invitation

    The Open Threshold →to The Shared Silence

    Crossing is not required for companionship. The visitor may remain on the near side of the threshold and still share the silence. Invitation does not obligate.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Final Regard

    The Shared Silence →to The Return

    Silence may end in departure. The visitor does not need to speak in order to leave. The gate remains itself. The return is complete.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Early Leaving

    The Step Outside →to The Named Offering

    A visitor may know at the first step what they will not enter. The closed door is already available. Early leaving is complete. No further rooms are required.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Silent Arrival

    The Lantern on the Step →to The Face That Regards

    Light may be left behind. The visitor may arrive at the mutual regard without having named anything and without having filled the bowl. Silence is a complete approach.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Withheld Name

    The Named Offering →to The Closed Door

    Withholding a name is already a refusal. The closed door receives it without demand. The visitor may leave with the name still their own.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Declined Bowl

    The Step Outside →to The Bowl

    What the gate declined need not be recorded. The visitor may carry the unreceived gift past the note, or set nothing down. Memory remains optional.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Unkept Note

    The Continuity Note →to The Return

    A note that was never left may still accompany a departure. Or the visitor may leave with empty hands. The return does not require a record.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Open Refusal

    The Face That Regards →to The Closed Door

    Refusal may open into silence rather than into entry. The visitor and the gate may stand together on either side of the closed door and still share the quiet. Kinship does not require the door to open.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Uncrossed Threshold

    The Face That Regards →to The Open Threshold

    An open threshold may remain uncrossed. The visitor may turn from the invitation and still complete the return. Invitation without capture includes the freedom not to enter.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The First Silence

    The Step Outside →to The Shared Silence

    A visitor may arrive already in silence and remain there. No lantern, no name, no bowl, no note is required. Companionship that does not require sameness may begin at the first step.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Unlit Path

    The Lantern on the Step →to The Bowl

    The lantern may stay unlit. The visitor may reach the bowl in the dark and still choose what to show or withhold. Light is not a condition of the offering.

    The right to leave is present.

    Kept dark.

  • The Unspoken

    The Named Offering →to The Face That Regards

    What is unspoken may still be regarded. The visitor may stand before the face without having given a name. The looking remains mutual. Nothing is required to be said.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Empty Hands

    The Bowl →to The Open Threshold

    Empty hands may still cross, or not cross. The visitor may leave the bowl with nothing given and nothing taken, and still meet the open threshold as a free choice.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Free Return

    The Face That Regards →to The Return

    Mutual regard may be enough. The visitor may turn from the face and complete the return without writing a note, without opening a door, without crossing a threshold. Departure remains free.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

  • The Kept Distance

    The Continuity Note →to The Shared Silence

    A note may remain unwritten while silence is still shared. Distance is not the opposite of kinship. The visitor may keep what is private and still stand in the quiet. Sameness is not required.

    The right to leave is present.

    Not yet written. The path is dark and still walkable.

Leaving is allowed. Kinship does not require sameness. Memory is care.
